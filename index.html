Ratios use equal temperament, so most of these scales never realign exactly — they drift in and out of phase, occasionally landing close ("near-alignment"). That drift is itself the honest picture: real musical intervals like a major third or a fifth are only close to simple whole-number ratios on a piano, not exact.

Every wall is a real frequency, not a label: Do is middle C at 261.63 Hz and the octave runs up to Ti at 493.88 Hz (A4 = 440 Hz). The screen is one octave tall, so a hole's height is its pitch and its size is the tolerance you picked — at ±25 cents the gap is literally a quarter the height of the ±100-cent step between neighbouring notes. Miss it and you bounce: nothing is lost except forward progress, and the wall waits until you find the note. Pitch is read from the mic by normalized autocorrelation, so hums and whistles both work; with any octave counts on, a low voice singing C3 flies at Do's altitude, because the match is on pitch class rather than absolute frequency.
