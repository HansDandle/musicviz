Ratios use equal temperament, so most of these scales never realign exactly — they drift in and out of phase, occasionally landing close ("near-alignment"). That drift is itself the honest picture: real musical intervals like a major third or a fifth are only close to simple whole-number ratios on a piano, not exact.

Each hurdle is a real frequency, not a label: Do is middle C at 261.63 Hz and the octave runs up to Ti at 493.88 Hz (A4 = 440 Hz). Pitch is read from the mic by normalized autocorrelation, so hums and whistles both work — with any octave counts on, a low voice singing C3 clears a C4 hurdle, because the match is on pitch class, not absolute frequency.
